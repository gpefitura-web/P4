# Cover letter (draft for the author to edit; English)

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Can critical slowing down indicators detect the loss of a stellar activity cycle? Size, power and positive controls on annual S-index series", for publication in New Astronomy.

Rolling-window indicators of critical slowing down (the lag-1 autocorrelation and the variance of a time series) are proposed as early warnings of abrupt transitions, and stellar activity cycles that switch off, as the Sun's did in the Maunder minimum, are an obvious candidate. We test whether the indicators can see such a loss in the long annual Ca II H and K (S index) records of Mount Wilson, in the solar record, and in sunspot group numbers. The paper is observational and about method: before any claim about stars, it measures the size and power of the surrogate test, applies it to three dynamical models in which a cycle is lost, and checks how an instrumental step and the length of the record change the answer.

The main findings are negative and calibrated. In the models, the loss of a cycle lowers the variance and the autocorrelation, so the one-sided hypothesis of slowing down has the wrong sign for this kind of transition; the test detects the fall of the amplitude, not slowing down. For a true rise of the autocorrelation the test has little power at the lengths of the available stellar records, and the paper gives the design curve for records of 30 to 200 years. The scan of 23 stars, the Sun and the Dalton minimum shows no excess of rejections in either tail, and the one star with a candidate transition, HD 166620, shows a lower variability in its grand minimum than in its second cycle minimum for every instrumental noise we assumed, and than in its first cycle minimum only if that noise is small.

Two hypotheses were declared before any indicator was computed on a real star; every analysis added afterwards is labeled in the text as decided after the stellar runs and exploratory, with the decision log in the repository. All numbers, tables and figures are generated by code that is deposited with the input data (except one third-party sunspot file, which the code reads from its distributor) and reproduces the manuscript from a clean copy.

This work has not been published before and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests. The declarations required by the journal, including the statement on generative AI, are provided through the submission system.
